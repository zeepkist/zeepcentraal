Good tracks combine readable racing lines, consistent flow, useful checkpoints, deliberate scenery, and sensible performance budgets.

## Beginner

- Read and learn the controls. Tips appear in the top-left corner the first time you enter the Level Editor.
- Take inspiration from everything you find fun, attractive, or beautiful.
- Build the track first and decoration last.
- Do not stay focused on only one project until it is finished. You will probably miss problems or errors on your map. Switch project at least twice a week.
- Try changing the weather or time of day, or rotate your whole map. It will change the look of your map.
- A good flow makes a good track.
- You have many paints with different properties. Try them.
- Mods will always be your allies. Try BlueprintsX and GridScaler to scale your blocks up or down.
- Checkpoints help racers and can act as anti-cheat measures. Try to place them every 10-20s.
- Signs are important, especially for blind turns, jumps, and hard-to-read turns.
- Add a booster in front of jumps at the lowest speed possible to achieve them. This prevents beginners from failing jumps.
- Scaling up rocks, trees, and other objects is useful for scenery.
- You do not need details everywhere. If racers cannot see it, you do not need it.
- Divide levels into folders such as ZSL, Banners, and Buildings. They will be easier to find and use fewer resources on your PC.
- Primitive blocks are useful. The pivot block is especially valuable.
- Thumbnails are important because they introduce your track. Save your thumbnail from a good viewpoint.

## Advanced

- Configure BlueprintsX to fit your build style. Some settings are not activated by default.
- If you want to build something huge, build it tiny first, then scale it up.
- If you want to build something small, build it big first, then scale it down.
- Try Vertex Snapping, Level Editor Trails, Area of Effect, Podium Preview, and Save States. Keep the tools that fit your workflow.
- Custom checkpoints can bring your map together.
- Make and save objects you like as BlueprintsX blueprints or separate levels. It will save a lot of time while building.
- Do not use the flat wall block to make walls. It is difficult to use with scaling. Use the cube primitive instead; it works better and has fewer polygons.
- Reduce block count. High block density is a major source of lag.
- For advanced thumbnails, replace your track thumbnail with one of your choice. Give it the same name and file type as the old thumbnail.

### Advanced tree trick

Use a cylinder with black and white vertical stripes using plastic paint. This technique saves blocks and helps older computers.

## Pro

- Build the landscape first, then the track layout, and decoration at the end. The track will look like it was already part of the landscape.
- Make fan particles disappear by making the fan very thin with a very high range. The particles take a long time to reach the end of the effect and reset. Example: fan height at 0.001 and range at 2000.
- Pro sausage trick: unknown.
- For a tube effect, use pipes, place them on their side, and squash them slightly. This technique was used in [“Emerald Coast Remaster”](/level/2101E0122EE6CF551881A99328D70F9D) and [“ZSL - Campagne”](/level/BEF169294CC95DF5369F51F11433A8F8).
- Reduce polygon count. The farther an object is from racers, the fewer polygons and details it needs.

::content-alert{type="reminder" title="Test with other players"}
A track that reads clearly to its builder may still surprise a first-time player. Ask racers with different skill levels to test before publishing.
::
